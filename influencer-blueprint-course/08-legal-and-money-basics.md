# Module 8 — Legal & Money Basics

**Student outcome:** knows the disclosure rules, can read a brand contract without
getting burned, and has entity/taxes on a checklist with a professional.

**Say this on camera, verbatim or close:** "We're not lawyers or accountants, and this
isn't legal or tax advice — it's the map of what to know and what to ask a
professional about. For your specific situation, hire the real thing. It's cheaper
than the mistake."

## Lesson 8.1 — Disclosure: #ad is not optional

**Teaching script:**

If you're paid — in money OR free product OR commissions — to talk about something,
you must disclose it clearly. In the US that's the FTC's endorsement rules; other
countries (UK ASA, etc.) have equivalents, and platforms layer their own tools on top.

The practical rules:
- Disclosure must be **hard to miss**: at the start of the caption (not buried in
  hashtags), said out loud in the video for video content, on-screen where relevant.
- "#ad" or "#sponsored" or "Paid partnership with X" — clear words. "#sp", "#collab",
  or a vague "thanks [brand]!" don't cut it.
- Use the platform's built-in paid-partnership label AND caption disclosure.
- Affiliate links count: "I earn a commission if you buy through my link."
- Free product counts: "The brand sent me this."

This isn't just fine-avoidance — audiences at this size can smell undisclosed ads, and
trust is the entire asset you monetize. Disclose proudly; it reads as professionalism.

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** Screenshots of YOUR properly disclosed sponsored posts as the
> examples. Link the current FTC endorsement guides page so students read the source.

**Action Step:** Audit your past sponsored/affiliate posts; fix any missing
disclosures. Write your standard disclosure line for captions and one for video.

## Lesson 8.2 — Reading a brand contract

**Teaching script:**

The five clauses to find in every brand agreement before signing:

1. **Deliverables & deadlines:** exactly what, exactly when, how many revisions
   (cap at 1–2 — "until satisfied" is unpaid infinite work).
2. **Usage rights:** where can they use your content, for how long, paid ads or
   organic only? "In perpetuity, all media" for free is a red flag — that's a paid
   line item (Module 4.3).
3. **Exclusivity:** what category, how long, and is it priced in?
4. **Payment terms:** amount, currency, schedule (50% upfront is reasonable to ask;
   net-30 is common), kill fee if they cancel late.
5. **Approval & morality clauses:** what lets them reject content or claw back
   payment? Vague "brand may terminate at its discretion without payment" — push back.

If a deal is worth four figures, having a lawyer spend 30 minutes on the contract is
cheap. And when the brand has no contract: send your own simple agreement (get a
lawyer to draft your reusable template once — it pays for itself forever).

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** A real (redacted) brand contract, marked up — highlight the five
> clauses and one clause you negotiated. Consider having YOUR lawyer produce a simple
> creator agreement template you're licensed to distribute to students; it's a
> massive value-add. Don't distribute a template no professional has reviewed.

**Action Step (worksheet):** The 5-clause checklist card — keep it open next time a
contract lands.

## Lesson 8.3 — Entity, banking, and taxes

**Teaching script:**

The map (professionals fill in the details for each student's country/state):

- **Entity:** many US creators form an LLC once income is real — liability separation
  and cleaner books. Whether/when it makes sense (and S-corp elections, etc.) is an
  accountant conversation, not a TikTok tip.
- **Banking:** separate business account from day one, even before an entity
  (Module 7.2). Every payment in, every expense out.
- **Income reporting:** brand-deal and platform income is taxable income. US brands
  will send 1099s over the threshold — but it's taxable whether or not a form shows
  up. Free products above token value can be taxable too; track what you receive.
- **Deductions:** equipment, software, home-office portion, contractors — track
  receipts as you go (a bookkeeping app or even a spreadsheet + folder of receipts).
- **Quarterly estimated taxes:** self-employment income usually means paying
  quarterly, not once in April. The set-aside habit from Module 7.2 is what makes
  this painless.

**When to get an accountant: the first year you cross meaningful creator income.
Sooner is better.**

> ✏️ **FILL IN (optional, high value):** A 15–20 minute recorded interview with a real
> CPA who works with creators, covering the five bullets above. Instantly the most
> credible lesson in the module — and it keeps you safely out of advice-giving.

**Action Step:** Book the accountant consult (many do a free first call). Bring:
income so far, income goal, and the question "entity now or later?"

## Lesson 8.4 — Protecting the business you're building (and selling honestly)

**Teaching script:**

Quick-hit list:

- **Own your audience:** the email list (Module 5) is the asset no platform ban can
  take. Export it regularly.
- **Trademark:** once your brand name has value, talk to an IP attorney about
  registering it.
- **Music & content rights:** commercial/sponsored content can't use the trending-audio
  library the way personal posts can — use commercially-licensed music on brand work.
- **Your own sales practices (this applies to YOU, students, when you sell):** only
  claim results that are real and documented; never present your best case as typical;
  honor your stated refund policy to the letter; real deadlines only. Consumer
  protection rules apply to creators selling courses and coaching exactly as they
  apply to brands — and beyond the rules, your audience is small enough that your
  reputation IS your distribution.

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** Any story from your own journey — a platform scare, a rights issue,
> a refund you honored that won a fan. End the module (and the course's teaching arc)
> on the theme that trust compounds.

**Action Step:** Export your email list today and calendar a monthly export. Write
your own results-claims rule of thumb for your sales content.
