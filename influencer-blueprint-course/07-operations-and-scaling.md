# Module 7 — Operations & Scaling

**Student outcome:** a simple operating rhythm (tools, money hygiene, weekly review),
knowing their first hire, and a clear picture of the three levers that scale past
$10k/month.

---

## Lesson 7.1 — The creator-business tool stack

**Teaching script:**

Keep it boring and cheap. One tool per job:

| Job | Tool category | Monthly cost band |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling content | Scheduler | $0–30 |
| Email list | Email platform | $0–50 |
| Landing/sales pages | Page builder | $0–40 |
| Payments | Checkout/processor | % of sales |
| Brand-deal tracking | Spreadsheet (free) | $0 |
| Calls | Calendar tool | $0–15 |
| Contracts | E-sign tool | $0–20 |

Total: a real creator business runs on well under $150/month. Don't buy tools as
procrastination.

**The named stack:** CapCut + Later (content), ConvertKit (email), Carrd (pages),
Stripe (payments), Google Sheets (deal tracker), Calendly (calls), Dropbox Sign or
DocuSign (contracts), Notion (idea bank + client docs). Roughly $60–100/month at
full build-out. If you ever use affiliate links when recommending tools, say so on
screen at that moment. **[TO RECORD]** a 5-minute tour of how the pieces connect.

**Action Step:** Fill any gaps in your own stack. Cancel anything you haven't opened
in 30 days.

## Lesson 7.2 — Money hygiene & the weekly review

**Teaching script:**

The habits that separate a business from a hobby:

- **Separate account:** all creator income in, all creator expenses out, from day one.
  (Module 8 covers entity and taxes.)
- **Set aside for taxes immediately:** move a fixed percentage of every payment into a
  savings sub-account the day it lands. Ask an accountant what % fits your situation —
  25–30% is a common starting point for self-employment income, but get real advice.
- **The 30-minute weekly review (same day every week):**
  1. Money: what came in, what's owed to you (chase net-30 invoices!), what went out
  2. Pipeline: brand-deal tracker + offer sales vs. last week
  3. Content: which posts drove DMs/clicks (not views)
  4. One decision: the single biggest thing to change next week

**The template (copy into Notion/Sheets — four boxes):** MONEY (in / owed / out) ·
PIPELINE (pitches sent, replies, calls booked, sales) · CONTENT (top post by
DMs-not-views, worst post, one-line why for each) · DECISION (the single change for
next week). Four boxes, 30 minutes, same day every week. **[TO RECORD]** yourself
running one real review, unedited.

**Action Step:** Do your first weekly review now, with whatever data you have. Put a
recurring 30-min block on the calendar.

## Lesson 7.3 — First hires: buying back your time

**Teaching script:**

Hire when a task is (a) repeatable, (b) not your unique skill, and (c) eating hours
you'd spend on money-making work. In order, for almost every creator:

1. **Video editor** (per-video or part-time): usually the first 10+ hours/week
   recovered. Your raw talking is the unique part; the cutting isn't.
2. **VA** (a few hrs/week): DM triage, brand-deal research, pipeline updates,
   scheduling.
3. **Later:** content strategist, community manager, bookkeeper.

How to hire small: start with one paid test project, clear written instructions
(record a Loom of you doing the task once), weekly check-in, pay promptly.

**The standard first-hire path (teach it straight):** a per-video editor, found by
asking creators one tier up who edits theirs. Test project of three videos at a flat
rate; the spec is a Loom of you editing one video your way. Typical short-form cost:
$10–30/video. The two ways it goes wrong: hiring for hours instead of deliverables,
and giving feedback scattered across DMs instead of one written doc.

**Action Step:** Write the job post for your first hire — even if you're not ready to
post it yet. Knowing the trigger point ("when I hit $X/mo or lose Y hrs/week") makes
the decision automatic later.

## Lesson 7.4 — The three scaling levers

**Teaching script:**

Once the machine works — offer sells, deals close, content ships weekly — there are
only three levers:

1. **More traffic:** collabs with peer creators, a second platform (repurpose, don't
   recreate), and eventually paid ads on your best-converting organic content.
   (Ads amplify a funnel that already converts; they can't fix one that doesn't.)
2. **Higher value per customer:** raise prices as proof accumulates; add an order
   bump / next-step offer; ascend best customers from low-ticket → mid → high-ticket.
   The ascension ladder is where mid-size creators quietly make most of their money.
3. **More leverage in delivery:** 1:1 → group programs; live → recorded + community;
   done-for-you → done-with-you. Same expertise, less of your time per dollar.

Pull ONE lever per quarter. Pulling all three at once breaks the machine you built.

**Nick's account maps to levers 1 and 3:** collabs and duet-bait expanded reach
(more traffic), and turning Nick's own songs into sounds other creators used was
pure leverage — content that recruits more content. The order that works: dominate
one platform organically, then repurpose to a second, and only once a funnel
demonstrably converts consider paid amplification of already-proven posts.

**Action Step (worksheet):** Write your next-quarter plan: which single lever, what
specific action, what number tells you it worked.
