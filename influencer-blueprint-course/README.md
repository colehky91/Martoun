# The Influencer Blueprint — Course Build Kit

This folder is the complete skeleton of your $1,000 blueprint course. Every module is
written A-to-Z; your job is to fill in the marked gaps with **your** stories, numbers,
screenshots, and examples. The structure, teaching flow, worksheets, and scripts are done.

**Who this course is for (the student):** creators with 25k–100k followers on TikTok /
Instagram / YouTube who are under-monetized and want a repeatable system to turn their
audience into income.

**The promise you're selling:** "A step-by-step system to go from 'I have followers' to
'I have a business' — pick your monetization path, package an offer, and make your first
(or next) $10k from the audience you already have."

## How to use this kit

1. Read `00-course-outline.md` first — it's the map of the whole product.
2. Go module by module. Everything you must write or record is marked like this:

   > ✏️ **FILL IN:** what goes here and why.

3. Anything not marked FILL IN is finished copy — use it as your teaching script
   verbatim, or say it in your own words on camera.
4. Each lesson ends with an **Action Step** — these become the student's workbook.
   Keep them; they're what makes the course feel worth $1,000.

## Production checklist (do these in order)

- [ ] **First:** you and Nick answer `15-brain-dump-worksheet.md` (voice memos are fine)
- [ ] Gather the receipts folder per `12-nick-case-study.md` Part 1 (screenshots, analytics)
- [ ] Fill files 12–14 from the brain-dump answers (or hand the answers to Claude to draft)
- [ ] Fill in every ✏️ block in modules 1–8 (start with Module 1 — it sets the tone)
- [ ] Record videos: one per lesson, 5–15 min each, talking-head + screen share
- [ ] Turn each module's Action Steps into a fillable PDF or Notion workbook
- [ ] Drop your real screenshots where marked (payouts, DMs, analytics) — proof sells
- [ ] Have someone outside the business watch Module 1 and tell you where they got bored
- [ ] Read `08-legal-and-money-basics.md` yourselves — the earnings-claims note applies
      to *your* sales page too: only show real, documented results, and never imply a
      typical student earns what your best example earned
