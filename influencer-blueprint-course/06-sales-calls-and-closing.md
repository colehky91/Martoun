# Module 6 — Sales Calls & Closing

**Student outcome:** can confidently run a 30-minute discovery call for a high-ticket
offer ($300+), handle the common objections, and follow up without chasing.

(Students selling only low-ticket products can skim this module — but brand-deal calls
use the same skills, so nobody should skip it entirely.)

---

## Lesson 6.1 — When you need a call, and the mindset

**Teaching script:**

Above roughly $300–500, most people won't buy from a page alone — they need a
conversation. A sales call is not a performance; it's a diagnosis. Your job is to find
out whether they have a problem you can genuinely solve, and to tell them the truth
about that either way. The moment you try to convince everyone, you convert no one.

The posture: **doctor, not vendor.** A doctor asks questions, diagnoses, and
prescribes — and refuses to prescribe when it doesn't fit.

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** Your first-sales-call horror story. Everyone has one. Then contrast
> with what a good call feels like now. Vulnerability here buys trust for the rest of
> the module.

**Action Step:** Book your calendar tool (Calendly or similar), 30-minute slots, with
2–3 qualifying questions on the booking form (goal, current situation, budget range).

## Lesson 6.2 — The 30-minute call framework

**Teaching script:**

Five phases, with time boxes:

1. **Open (2 min):** rapport + set the frame: "I'll ask questions about where you are
   and what you're going for; if it looks like a fit I'll show you how the program
   works, and if not I'll point you somewhere better. Sound good?"
2. **Current state (8 min):** their situation, in detail and in numbers. "What have
   you tried? What happened?"
3. **Desired state (5 min):** what they actually want and *why now*. Get specific —
   "what does good look like in 6 months?"
4. **The gap + prescription (10 min):** replay what you heard ("so you're at A, you
   want B, and the thing in the way is C"). Then present the offer as the bridge —
   only the parts relevant to their C.
5. **Close (5 min):** state the price plainly, then stop talking. Silence is not a
   problem. Then: "what questions do you have?"

Rule: they should talk 60%+ of the call. If you're monologuing, you're losing.

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** A recorded roleplay of the full call — you and your business partner
> playing coach/prospect. This recording IS the lesson; the framework above is just
> its outline. Consider also including a "bad call" version for contrast.

**Action Step (worksheet):** Write your own phase 1 frame and your phase 4 replay
sentence, word for word. Practice aloud twice.

## Lesson 6.3 — Objections: the big four

**Teaching script:**

Objections are unanswered questions, not rejections. The big four and honest
responses:

1. **"I can't afford it."** Clarify which it is: a priority question or a real
   constraint. "Totally fair — is it that the money isn't there, or that you're not
   sure this is worth it?" If money truly isn't there, do NOT pressure — point them to
   your low-ticket option or free content. High-pressure selling to people who can't
   afford it ruins reputations and deserves to.
2. **"I need to think about it."** "Of course. Usually that means one of: timing,
   money, or you're not sure it'll work for you. Which is it?" Then answer the real
   one.
3. **"I need to ask my partner/spouse."** Respect it. Offer to send a one-pager they
   can share, and book the follow-up before hanging up.
4. **"Will this work for ME?"** The proof question. Answer with the closest real case
   study you have — and if you don't have one for their situation, say so and let
   them decide with accurate information.

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** The objections you ACTUALLY hear in your niche and your real
> responses. Record a rapid-fire roleplay of all four.

**Action Step:** Script your responses to the big four in your own voice.

## Lesson 6.4 — Follow-up and the no-chase rule

**Teaching script:**

Most high-ticket sales close in follow-up, not on the call. The system:

- End every non-closing call with a scheduled next touch ("I'll check in Thursday —
  does that work?"). Never "let me know!"
- Follow-up sequence: day 2 (recap + one-pager), day 5 (relevant case study or
  answer to their specific objection), day 10 (honest final check-in: "closing the
  loop — should I take you off my list for this round?").
- Three touches, then stop. People who feel hunted don't buy, and they tell others.

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** Your actual follow-up message templates (also going in the swipe
> file) and one story of a sale that closed on touch three.

**Action Step:** Set up follow-up templates in your notes app / CRM so every call ends
with the sequence armed.
