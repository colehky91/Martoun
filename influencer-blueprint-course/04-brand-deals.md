# Module 4 — Brand Deals

**Student outcome:** media kit and rate card built, target list of 50 brands, first 20
pitches sent, and a negotiation framework for when replies come in.

This is the fastest path to money at 25k–100k followers, which is why it comes before
selling your own offer.

---

## Lesson 4.1 — Why brands want YOU (not the million-follower account)

**Teaching script:**

Brands have figured out that mid-size creators ("micro" and "mid-tier") often deliver
better engagement per dollar than celebrities. Your pitch angle is exactly that: a
tight, trusting audience in a specific niche. You're not competing with huge accounts —
you're a different product: authenticity and niche depth at an accessible price.

What brands actually buy: audience match (their customer = your follower), engagement
quality (real comments, not bot hearts), and content skill (can you make an ad that
doesn't look like an ad).

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** Your best brand-deal proof — deal screenshots, payout screenshots,
> or client examples (amounts redacted or not, your call — but real). If your proof is
> thinner here, use documented public examples and say so honestly.

**Action Step:** Write your one-line brand pitch: "I reach [WHO] who care about
[WHAT], with [engagement stat] engagement."

## Lesson 4.2 — The media kit

**Teaching script:**

A media kit is a 3–5 page PDF (or Canva link) that answers a brand's questions before
they ask. Pages:

1. **Cover:** name, handle, niche, one killer stat, photo.
2. **Audience:** follower count per platform, demographics (age/gender/location from
   your analytics), engagement rate. Screenshot your real analytics — brands trust
   screenshots over typed numbers.
3. **Content & results:** 3–6 best posts with view/engagement numbers; past brand work
   if any (or best "ad-style" organic posts if none).
4. **Offerings & rates:** packages (next lesson).
5. **Contact.**

**Engagement rate math:** (avg likes + comments on last 10 posts) ÷ followers × 100.
Above ~3% is a selling point at this size — feature it.

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** Your own media kit as the worked example (PDF in the course assets),
> plus a Canva template link students can duplicate. This template is one of the
> highest-perceived-value assets in the whole course.

**Action Step:** Build your media kit from the template. Deliverable: a shareable link.

## Lesson 4.3 — Pricing: the rate card

**Teaching script:**

Creators at this size undercharge, badly. Baseline framework (teach the *method*, not
magic numbers):

- **Starting anchor:** a commonly used industry starting point is roughly $100 per
  10k followers per dedicated post — that is a *floor*, not a rate. From there,
  multiply for:
  - High-trust/high-value niche (finance, B2B, health): 2–5x
  - Strong engagement (>3–4%): 1.5–2x
  - Deliverable weight: video > photo; dedicated post > mention
- **Always price in usage rights:** if the brand wants to run your content as a paid
  ad ("whitelisting"/"usage"), that is a separate line item — commonly +30–100% of the
  content fee per period of use. Never give it away silently.
- **Exclusivity costs extra:** if they want you to avoid competitors for 90 days,
  charge for the deals you can't take.
- **Packages beat one-offs:** pitch a 3-post package at a modest discount — bigger
  invoice for you, better results for them (repetition converts).

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** Real rate examples — what you or your clients actually charged for
> specific deliverables at specific follower counts, and what the brand said yes to.
> 2–3 real data points make this lesson land harder than any formula.

**Action Step (worksheet):** Build your rate card: 1 post / 3-post package / story
package / UGC-only (content without posting) — with usage rights priced separately.

## Lesson 4.4 — Outreach: building the pipeline

**Teaching script:**

Don't wait for inbound. The system:

1. **Target list (50 brands):** brands you already use + brands sponsoring creators
   your size in your niche (check who your peer accounts tag with #ad) + smaller/DTC
   brands whose ads you see in your own feed. Direct-to-consumer brands with active
   ad spend say yes fastest.
2. **Find the human:** look for marketing/influencer/partnerships contacts on the
   brand site or LinkedIn; DM the brand account if you can't find email. Email > DM
   for anything above a small deal.
3. **The pitch (short!):** who you are, one audience-match sentence, one proof point,
   one specific idea for THEM, ask for a call or their brief. Full scripts in the
   swipe file (`09-templates-and-scripts.md`).
4. **Volume + follow-up:** 20 pitches, follow up twice (day 4 and day 10). Most yeses
   arrive on follow-up. Track everything in a simple sheet.

Expected math to set honestly: cold outreach at this level might convert 2–10% to
conversations. That's why the number is 20, not 5.

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** A real successful pitch you've sent (screenshot) and the reply. Plus
> your tracking-sheet template (Google Sheets link) with columns: brand / contact /
> date pitched / follow-ups / status / value.

**Action Step:** Build the 50-brand list, send the first 10 pitches this week, 10 next
week. Log everything.

## Lesson 4.5 — Negotiation & red flags

**Teaching script:**

When a brand replies:

- **Never accept the first number.** A counter of 20–30% with a reason ("that rate
  doesn't include usage rights — with 30-day usage it's $X") is normal and expected.
- **"We pay in product"** — fine only if you'd have bought the product anyway and the
  deal is small; otherwise: "I love the product — my content rates are separate, and
  here's my kit."
- **Get it in writing.** Deliverables, deadlines, revisions (cap at 1–2), usage,
  exclusivity, payment terms (50% upfront, or net-15/30 on delivery). Module 8 covers
  reading their contract.
- **Red flags:** pay-to-play ("buy product to be an ambassador"), unlimited revisions,
  perpetual usage rights for free, exclusivity with no fee, "exposure" as payment.

> ✏️ **FILL IN:** One real negotiation story with the actual back-and-forth — where
> they started, what you countered, where it landed. And one deal you walked away
> from and why.

**Action Step:** Write your two counter-scripts (rate too low / product-only offer) so
you're never negotiating live without a plan.
