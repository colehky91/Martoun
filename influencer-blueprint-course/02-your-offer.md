# Module 2 — Your Offer

**Student outcome:** ONE monetization path chosen, one offer defined (promise,
deliverable, price), and early validation from their actual audience.

---

## Lesson 2.1 — The four monetization paths

**Teaching script:**

There are only four ways a mid-size creator makes real money. Everything else is a
variation:

| Path | What it is | Best when… | Speed to first $ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Brand deals** | Companies pay you to feature them | Any niche brands spend in; steady posting | Fast (2–6 weeks) |
| **2. Digital products** | Course, ebook, preset pack, template, community | You teach/show a skill people want | Medium (builds an asset) |
| **3. Services & coaching** | 1:1 or group coaching, done-for-you work | High-trust niche, you have real expertise | Fast, but sells your time |
| **4. Affiliate** | Commission for products you'd recommend anyway | Product-heavy niches (tech, beauty, home) | Fast, low ceiling alone |

The mistake is doing all four at once. Pick ONE primary path for the next 90 days.
Affiliate can layer on top of anything; the other three need focus.

**How to pick:** trust level of your niche × what your audit said your audience wants ×
how much time you actually have. Brand deals need the least infrastructure — that's why
Module 4 comes before Module 5.

**Our answer:** with Nick we monetized the *audience asset itself* — streams — before
selling anything. The audience was the product; Spotify paid for the attention. For
most non-music creators the same logic points at brand deals first (fastest cash at
this size) while a product offer validates in the background. The one thing that
never works: doing everything at once — split focus shows up in the content within a
week.

**Action Step:** Choose your primary path and write one sentence on why. Commit for
90 days.

## Lesson 2.2 — Designing the offer (for paths 2 and 3)

**Teaching script:**

An offer is three decisions:

1. **The promise:** the specific outcome, for whom, in what timeframe. Use the
   positioning statement from Module 1 — the offer is just that sentence with a price.
2. **The deliverable:** the minimum format that delivers the promise. Don't build a
   40-video course when a 20-page guide + one template gets the result. Smaller ships
   faster and validates cheaper.
3. **The price:** anchor to the value of the outcome, not your hours. Starting bands
   that work at this audience size:
   - Low-ticket ($27–$97): impulse-buy digital products, sold from content directly
   - Mid-ticket ($197–$497): deeper program, needs a sales page (Module 5)
   - High-ticket ($1,000+): coaching/services, needs a call (Module 6)

Rule: your FIRST offer should be sellable within 30 days of starting this course.
Version 2 can be fancy.

**Worked example — this very course.** Promise: "the organic-growth system that took
Nick to 100M views and 60M streams, installed in your account in 30 days."
Deliverable: 8 modules + swipe file + 30-day plan — the minimum that delivers the
promise, not 40 hours of filler. Price: $1,000, anchored to what one brand deal or
one real month of growth returns, not to hours of video. And the coaching tier came
first: working with people directly is what taught us what the course needed to say.

Example first offers that fit this audience size:
- **Music:** $297 "Fan Machine" — 30 days of content systems to turn a release into a fanbase.
- **Fitness:** $97 8-week home program + form-check community.
- **Finance:** $197 "first $10k invested" course with a monthly live Q&A.
- **Beauty:** $47 routine-builder guide + product-picker quiz (volume play).
- **Any niche, high-ticket:** $1,500 done-with-you 6 weeks (needs Module 6 call skills).

**Action Step (worksheet):** Fill in the offer one-pager: Promise / Who it's for /
Deliverable / Price / Why they'd buy it from me.

## Lesson 2.3 — Validate before you build

**Teaching script:**

Never spend a month building something nobody asked for. Validation ladder — stop at
the first rung that fails and revise:

1. **Post the promise** as content ("I'm thinking of putting together X — who'd
   want that?"). Measure replies/DMs, not likes.
2. **DM your warmest 20** — people who consistently comment/reply. Ask what they
   struggle with in your topic. Their words become your sales copy.
3. **Presell it.** Offer founding-member pricing to a waitlist before the thing fully
   exists (be honest that it's coming, and deliver on time). 5–10 presales = build it.
   Zero presales = the offer or the promise is wrong, and you just saved a month.

**How this plays out in practice:** the "who'd want this?" post plus 20 warm DMs
tells you in 72 hours what a month of building can't. Expect most DMs to go
unanswered — five real replies is signal. The words people reply with become your
sales-page headline (Module 5). If presells land at zero, the market isn't broken —
the promise is. Rewrite it in their words and run it again. *(Screenshot the first
real validation run you do for a client and drop it in here — build the receipt
library as you go.)*

**Action Step:** Run rungs 1 and 2 this week. Record: how many replies, and the exact
words people used to describe their problem.
